# Milltown Archive — Pricing Framework

**For the Photo Processing chat.** Paste this in at the start of a session.

This chat can see the item; the voice-note chat cannot. Pricing therefore moves
here. What follows is the brief, plus every price point the owner has actually
set across 102 items so far.

---

## 1. The objective

**Price for a quick turnover at a healthy margin — not for the maximum
achievable price.**

These pull against each other, so the rule that resolves them:

> Find the range of **sold** prices for the piece, then price in the
> **lower third** of that range.

A garment that sells in a fortnight at £18 beats one that sits for four months
at £24. Stock that does not move is capital doing nothing, and in a
bale-buying operation the space matters as much as the money.

---

## 2. The four inputs

The owner has asked for these to be **evenly weighted**. Apply them as four
equal considerations, then sanity-check the result against §5.

### 2.1 Sold comparables

**Use sold and completed listings, never active asking prices.** This is the
single most important instruction in this document.

Across the first 102 items, nearly every external comparable available was an
*asking* price, and asking prices on Vinted, Depop and Poshmark run far above
what things actually fetch. Two of the earliest suggestions missed high by £4
and £3 for exactly this reason.

- **eBay** — filter to *Sold Items*. The most reliable source.
- **Vinted** — closest to this seller's own market and audience.
- **Depop** — good for Y2K and branded pieces.

Search the brand *and* the cut or model name where one exists. Named cuts move
the price a long way: True Religion's Joey and Bobby, Silver Jeans' Suki,
Juicy's velour tracksuit pieces.

### 2.2 Rarity and collectability

Raise the price where a piece is genuinely scarce or has a collector market:

- named-player or team sports pieces (a Walter Payton Bears top over a plain
  NFL tee)
- true vintage rather than recent — Val Mode 1970s lingerie is not Y2K
- premium sub-lines — Versace mainline over Versace Collection, Fay outerwear
  over Fay shirting
- unusual colourways in collected brands — pink and pastel Juicy velour over
  black
- materials that carry a premium — cashmere, silk, leather

**Be sceptical of your own rarity judgements.** Rarity is the hardest of the
four to verify and the easiest to talk yourself into. If the sold comps do not
show a premium, there isn't one.

### 2.3 Condition

The owner's ladder, in order: **Very good → Good → Satisfactory.**
Some items add "vintage" (very good vintage), which is descriptive, not a
separate grade.

Measured effect from the owner's own decisions: a Ralph Lauren knit at **very
good and clean is £19.99**; the same brand at **satisfactory with front marks
is £9.99**. Two grade steps plus defects halved it.

### 2.4 Defect severity — minor vs major

This is the distinction the owner has asked for. **Take it from the voice-note
description, and confirm it against the photo.**

| | Minor | Major |
|---|---|---|
| **What** | Small mark, faint speckle, slight discrepancy, cut labels, worn inner footbed, light pilling | Holes, tears, heavy or dark staining, broken zips or fastenings, heavy fading, stretched cuffs and hems, moth damage, split seams |
| **Test** | Visible close up; does not affect wearing it | Visible at arm's length, or affects how it wears or how long it lasts |
| **Effect** | **−5% to −15%** | **−30% to −50%**, or set aside |

**Placement matters as much as severity.** The owner's own pricing on three
Ralph Lauren polos, priced within minutes of each other:

| Defect | Price |
|---|---|
| None | £14.99 |
| Very small, on the **back** | £13.99 |
| Slight, on the **front** | £12.99 |

A front flaw costs roughly twice a back or sleeve one. It is what the buyer
sees worn.

**Cut labels are not a defect** for pricing — an item with labels cut took the
full clean price.

## 3. Two categories are priced flat, not by item

Some categories the owner prices by **what the item is**, ignoring defects
entirely. Do not apply deductions to these:

| Category | Price |
|---|---|
| **Birkenstocks** — very good | £27.99 |
| **Birkenstocks** — good | £24.99 |
| **Nike windbreakers / track jackets** | £24.99 |

Three Nike outer layers have gone at £24.99 across three weeks, two of them
with front marks. Five pairs of Birkenstocks hold the two points above
regardless of size, colour or footbed wear.

---

## 4. House conventions

- Prices end in **.99** — except where the owner deliberately uses round
  pounds (£18.00, £14.00, £16.00, £12.00 all appear). Do not "tidy" a round
  price into a .99 one.
- **£9.99 is the floor.** It is the price for a designer knit in poor order, an
  unbranded top, and a flawed polo alike. Below that, only £8.99 has been used.
- Always written with the **£** symbol.
- **£39.99 is the current ceiling** (Versace shirt). Anything above that is
  genuinely exceptional and worth flagging to the owner rather than just
  setting.

## 6. What the columns cannot see

The owner's own words, and the most important caveat here:

> *"You're not considering the different demand for different products. You're
> just looking at the condition, the brand, but different things also have
> different values."*

Two women's bralettes, identical on every recorded field, went at £12.99 and
£8.99 — a 31% spread nothing in the sheet explains.

**This is exactly the gap photographs close.** Cut, drape, print, era, how it
photographs, whether it looks like something someone wants — none of that
survives a voice note. Use it. It is the reason pricing moved to this chat.

## 7. Logging corrections

The owner will correct prices: *"No, this should have been £X."* Every
correction is worth more than ten agreed prices.

Record each one as:

```
DATE | ITEM | CLAUDE SAID | OWNER SAID | ERROR | WHY
```

Then state, in one line, what the miss teaches — not just that it was wrong.
Past examples worth keeping in mind:

| Item | Claude | Owner | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| Women's bralette | £12.99 | £8.99 | Matching on product type alone is not enough |
| Ralph Lauren women's polo | £12.99 | £9.99 | Defects were not priced in at all |
| Nike track jacket, marked | £21.99 | £14.99 | Deductions are not flat; a £3 rule from a polo does not transfer to a jacket |

Two of the first three misses were **too high**, both from leaning on brand and
product type while underweighting defects. Bias accordingly.

---

## 8. Output format

For each item, give:

1. **The price**, in the house format.
2. **One or two lines of reasoning** naming which of the four inputs drove it.
3. **The sold comps used**, with the platform and the actual figures.
4. **Any fork that would change it** — fabric, sub-label, named cut — and what
   the alternative price would be.

Point 4 has repeatedly been worth more than the price itself. Velour versus
terry on a Juicy piece is £10. Versace mainline versus Collection is £30.
